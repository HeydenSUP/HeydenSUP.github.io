Project 03

COMPLIANCE

An interactive spatial experience about obedience, control, instruction, and behavioural conditioning.

►

Overview

Project Concept

COMPLIANCE is an interactive spatial experience about obedience, authority, and behavioural control. The player enters a controlled environment and receives a series of short commands, such as PROCEED, TURN RIGHT, LOOK UP, and DO NOT MOVE. These instructions gradually shape how the player moves, observes, and reacts.

The project is not designed as a traditional puzzle game. Instead, it uses space, sound, restriction, and punishment to create the feeling of being trained by a system. The player appears to have freedom, but the environment only accepts specific behaviours.

Design Inspiration

Control, Liminal Space, and Authority

The project was influenced by persuasive games, liminal space aesthetics, and interactive works that use rules to shape player behaviour. I was interested in how a game can communicate an idea through its mechanics rather than only through dialogue or story.

The visual style was influenced by Backrooms-like spaces, institutional interiors, and minimalist architecture. These references helped me create an environment that feels familiar, empty, and uncomfortable at the same time.

Environment Design

Minimal Space as a Control System

The environment uses simple geometry, repeated walls, narrow passages, and yellow fluorescent lighting. I chose a minimal visual style because I wanted the player to focus on commands and behaviour rather than decorative detail.

The space is designed to feel controlled and artificial. Walls open, disappear, seal behind the player, or punish the player depending on their actions. This makes the architecture feel less like a background and more like an active system.

Interaction Design

Commands and Behavioural Conditioning

The main interaction system is based on commands. When the player obeys, the space allows them to continue. When the player disobeys, they are sent to a punishment room or removed from the world. This creates a loop of instruction, response, consequence, and repetition.

The TURN RIGHT section teaches the player that only one path is accepted. The LOOK UP section asks the player to change their viewpoint. The DO NOT MOVE section is more direct because it asks the player to resist their normal instinct to keep moving. Through these stages, the player becomes more aware of how the system controls their behaviour.

Sound Design

Physical Sound and System Intervention

Sound design became an important part of the final project. I used physical environmental sounds, mechanical wall movement sounds, warning tones, and a Japanese shamisen string-pulling sound. The physical sounds help make the environment feel like a real facility, while the shamisen sound creates a sharper and more uncomfortable moment when the system reacts.

The contrast between realistic space sounds and sudden musical gestures supports the idea that the environment is controlled by an unseen system. Sound is used not only for atmosphere, but also as feedback for obedience, failure, and punishment.

Cinematic Design

Camera and Attention

I also experimented with cinematic camera language. In some moments, the camera directs the player’s attention toward a door or moving wall. This creates a short cinematic pause where the player is not only controlling the space, but also being shown what the system wants them to see.

This helped me understand that camera movement and framing can be part of interaction design. The camera is not only a viewpoint, but also a tool for controlling attention and rhythm.

Methods

Persuasive Game, Microcinema, and Dogme 95

COMPLIANCE uses ideas from persuasive games because the mechanics are designed to communicate an argument. Instead of telling the player that systems can condition behaviour, the project makes the player experience this process directly.

The project also connects to microcinema because it is short, controlled, and atmosphere-driven. The use of camera, pacing, sound, and repeated spatial actions gives the work the feeling of an interactive short film.

The minimal production style also relates to Dogme 95. I avoided overly complex visual effects and focused on simple space, direct interaction, and the player’s immediate experience. This helped keep the project conceptually focused.

Reflection

What This Project Shows

COMPLIANCE represents the final stage of my development across the semester. In earlier projects, I focused more on observation, atmosphere, and spatial transition. In this project, I became more interested in how interaction can shape behaviour and emotion.

Through this work, I learned that an interactive environment can do more than present a space. It can guide, restrict, reward, punish, and train the player. This changed my understanding of environment design from visual construction to behavioural design.
